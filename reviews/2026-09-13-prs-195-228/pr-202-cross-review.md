## Specialized critical values

The first n power sums force the exact characteristic polynomial by Newton identities. The additional square-free polynomial q_lambda(A)=0 forces semisimplicity, so the zero set is precisely the single complex similarity orbit with the prescribed multiplicities. This is essential: trace equations alone would allow Jordan blocks. Specializing the eigenvalues and their equality pattern before elimination correctly includes repeated, zero and scalar spectra.

That semisimple similarity orbit is smooth, with tangent directions [X,A]. The permanental adjoint has the correct transposed-minor convention, giving d(per)_A(E)=tr(C(A)E) and d(per)_A([X,A])=tr([A,C(A)]X). For Hermitian A, C(A) is Hermitian. A unitary stationary point therefore has skew-Hermitian T=[A,C(A)] and tr(T²)=−||T||_F²=0; all Hermitian maxima lie in the complex critical locus. The unitary orbit is compact and has no boundary, including its lower-dimensional repeated-spectrum cases.

The critical locus can be positive dimensional, but it has finitely many irreducible components. On the smooth dense part of each component, the differential of the permanent is zero because it already vanishes on the containing smooth orbit's tangent space. In characteristic zero, zero differential in an irreducible function field over an algebraically closed constant field forces constancy. Regularity extends constancy across each component. Hence there are finitely many critical values, even without finitely many critical matrices.

The polynomial vanishing on this finite value set has a power in the graph ideal by the Nullstellensatz over the algebraic closure. Extension of a Gröbner basis preserves its leading monomials and elimination property, so the elimination ideal over F=Q(lambda) is nonzero as well. The Hermitian maximizer is an actual graph zero, making that ideal proper and ensuring its square-free monic generator retains M as a root. This handles ideal nonradicality without assuming the defining equations form a reduced scheme. Real roots not realized on the Hermitian orbit can occur; the later selector does not assume otherwise.

## Moment formula and all orders

The normalized symmetric tensor w_n has norm one and gives per(A)=<w_n,A^tensor(n)w_n>. Therefore the objective is real and lies in [0,L^n] on the PSD orbit. For moment p, the row subgroup H symmetrizes each length-n block, while the column subgroup K stabilizes the repeated basis tensor; H∩K is trivial. The factor sqrt(|H|) normalizes the symmetrized vector exactly. The central-character-projector calculation produces the stated coefficient f^nu/(np)! times the double H,K character sum, with no missing factorial. Those coefficients are squared norms of orthogonal projections and sum to one.

I independently checked [Collins–Śniady, arXiv math-ph/0402073, Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0402073). They supply the Schur–Weyl decomposition and Haar conditional expectation used here. The decomposition restricts partition length to at most the matrix dimension; it does not require np≤n. On each irreducible unitary factor the averaged conjugate is its trace divided by dimension, giving s_nu(lambda)/s_nu(1^n). The denominator is positive, including at singular spectra, because it depends only on the representation dimension. The moment is thus a finite polynomial in the eigenvalues with rational coefficients. Jacobi–Trudi, hook formulas and the stated character recursion specify finite evaluation without dividing by eigenvalue differences.

## Finite selector

The tensor telescoping bound gives |f(U)−f(V)|≤2nB||U−V||_F, where B=L^n. The packing argument in real ambient dimension 2n² gives the stated positive lower bound on every Haar ball: an r-separated finite net has bounded size by disjoint ambient-ball volumes, and its equal-mass group balls cover the group. This reasoning uses only compactness and unitary invariance; it does not assume uniform coordinates or full ambient-dimensional Haar density.

Let rho be the smallest gap in the candidate list. A radius rho/(8nB) ball around a maximizer has f≥M−rho/4 and Haar mass at least 1/Q with Q=(1+16n²B/rho)^(2n²). Thus M≤rho/4+Q^(1/p) T^(1/p); if the local bound is negative this inequality is immediate, so there is no illicit raising of a negative bound. The specified integer b bounds Q by 2^(2n²b), while h=ceil(4B/rho) and Bernoulli's inequality give (1+rho/(4B))^h≥2. With p=2n²bh this yields 0≤M−T^(1/p)≤rho/2.

Every candidate strictly below M is at most M−rho and hence strictly below T^(1/p). Every candidate at or above M is at least T^(1/p). Therefore the first root whose pth power is at least T is exactly M, and the displayed indicator sum telescopes to M. This proves selection even in the presence of infeasible real complex-critical values, equal moments at a root, or zero candidate roots. All-zero spectra and a single surviving candidate are separately covered; n=1 and scalar spectra collapse correctly to a single value.

## Arbitrary real inputs and executable scope

The ordered field generated by finitely many actual real eigenvalues is Archimedean. With the explicitly stated exact arithmetic and comparison model, coefficients and equality patterns can be processed by Gröbner elimination. Square-free univariate roots can be encoded and isolated by Sturm operations over that field. Since finitely many distinct roots have positive separation, rational refinement eventually gives disjoint brackets and a positive rational lower bound for their gaps. The manuscript's safe-gap variant allows that lower bound in place of computing rho explicitly. Exact signs of T−r_j^p are algebraic-root comparisons; no numerical approximation oracle is being silently assumed. For arbitrary unspecified real numbers, the claim is relative to exact access to those inputs, as the README expressly states; it is not an algorithm deciding their relations from finite-precision measurements.

The inspected reference code accepts rational spectra, performs the specialized ideal and square-free elimination, and refuses to return a value if a resource or algebraic-comparison guard is exceeded. Its early exit retains candidates at least the first moment, a certified lower bound on M, and its isolating-interval gap is a conservative lower bound. These are valid safe variants. No claim of a successful arbitrary-order prototype follows from the theorem or small cases.
