# Scope and status

## Established in this package

The Krause three-point pair has exact unitary-orbit distance sqrt(27/13) in every finite amplification. Its bottleneck eigenvalue-matching distance is sqrt(28/13), so the amplification theorem is not a consequence of equality with spectral matching.

Every equality-attaining amplified unitary for that pair has the form V(R tensor I_k)W, where V and W commute with all three spectral projections of A. This follows from the exact positive Gram certificate and the verified degree-four matrix-algebra relation kernel.

The manuscript also proves amplification invariance for pairs on parallel lines, perpendicular lines, or concentric circles, and for pairs whose matching distance is below half the spectral gap of one input. These are geometric special cases or consequences of standard spectral-variation results; no novelty claim is made for them. The previously supplied one-two-point-spectrum theorem is credited, not claimed as a new result.

General lower bounds, a quantitative neighborhood estimate, and a necessary noncommutativity condition for a smallest-amplification counterexample are proved.

## Not established

The universal reverse inequality

    delta_n(A,B) <= delta_nk(A tensor I_k, B tensor I_k)

has not been established for arbitrary normal A and B. No normal finite-dimensional counterexample has been certified. The single-pair sum-of-squares certificate is specialized to its stated eigenvalues and trace constraints; substituting arbitrary spectra is not justified.

Numerical searches do not prove that their reported base-size values are global minima. A difference between two such numerical values would not, by itself, establish an amplification counterexample.

## Repository interpretation

The evidence supports additional partial results, not a change of SP-09 to Fully resolved. The current repository already labels SP-09 Partially resolved. This package is not a repository edit or an assertion that its new certificate has received independent review.
