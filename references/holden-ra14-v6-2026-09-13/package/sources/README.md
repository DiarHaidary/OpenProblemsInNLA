# Primary sources and dependency roles

`primary_sources.json` records original titles, authors, versions, source locations, dates, and the precise role of each reference. The report contains numbered citations and a bibliography. No complete external papers or font files are redistributed here.

The public RA-14 statement was read directly through its public website/raw source, not through a GitHub connector. Its requirements are stronger than a lower bound for one Krylov implementation: individual products are charged, queries are arbitrary and adaptive, and every finite parameter triple and every fixed input must be covered.

Bakshi-Narayanan supplies the question's antecedent and the adaptive-to-Krylov comparison. Its general lifting has quadratic overhead. The new proof does not discard that overhead. Tropp and Urschel provide context for block-Krylov and finite-dimensional polynomial error analyses; their statements are not asserted to resolve the current all-adaptive target.

Braverman-Hazan-Simchowitz-Woodworth is cited for antecedent Wishart Schur-complement methodology. The shifted posterior, its first-exit identity, and the averaged algorithm are proved in the new manuscript rather than imported from that paper. Rudelson-Vershynin remains an explicit external probability-theorem dependency only of the inherited v5 lower bound.

The later block-size paper was checked in both its initially retrieved v1 and the subsequently verified v2 (October 21, 2025). Its logarithmic gap/conditioning qualifiers and its input-perturbation mechanism are not silently removed. A known noncommuting perturbation can take a method outside the fixed-span query definition used in this note. The HTML rendering's later body date is not used as evidence of a separate arXiv revision.

The source review is bounded. It does not establish mathematical priority for the new lemmas, exhaust every paper, or certify the generated proofs. The central conclusion about incomplete RA-14 scope follows from the explicit gap in this package, not simply from the public repository's status label.
