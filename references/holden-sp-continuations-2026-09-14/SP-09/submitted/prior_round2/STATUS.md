# Scope and status

## Established here

An existential open neighborhood of the reference ordered spectral pair in `C^3 x C^3` is amplification invariant for every finite amplification. The six eigenvalues can be perturbed independently. The orbit distance remains nonmatching after shrinking the neighborhood. The scalar optimizer is unique modulo diagonal phases, and every amplified optimizer is its tensor repetition modulo spectral-block unitaries.

The local proof is based on a universal coefficient map of rank exactly 107 in 109 real trace coordinates. An explicit minor is verified over the integers and modulo a prime. The reference face derivative has rank three, the moment derivative has rank four, and the unique trace-one stationary density has positive eigenvalues 5/28 and 23/28. These facts force top singular multiplicity two at all nearby base minimizers and permit continuous transport of a positive trace certificate.

For the original spectral pair, the archive proves a dimension-independent normalized Hilbert–Schmidt stability estimate with the explicit constant 2,705,974. Its proof uses exact rational relation bounds and constructive polar alignment.

The preceding exact reference-pair theorem and equality classification remain included and are reverified, not replaced by numerical evidence.

## Not established

The universal SP-09 reverse inequality for arbitrary normal matrices and arbitrary base dimension is not established. No normal-matrix counterexample is certified.

No numerical radius for the open neighborhood is established. The continuity proof is not a numerical validation of any proposed radius. It does not cover arbitrary splittings of the repeated eigenvalues in dimension 3k.

No global continuation across parameter-space boundaries is established. Singular-face multiplicity, coefficient-map rank, or strict Gram positivity may change outside the neighborhood. Openness near one example does not imply global invariance.

The quantitative theorem gives normalized Hilbert–Schmidt closeness, not operator-norm closeness. Its explicit constant is not sharp.
