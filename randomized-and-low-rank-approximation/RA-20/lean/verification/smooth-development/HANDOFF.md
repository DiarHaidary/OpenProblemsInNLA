## Actual algebraic argument

The polynomial ring localized at the preimage of the quotient point maps onto the actual local ring of `C[a,b,c]/(abc)`. `abcLocalizationMap_surjective` and `abcLocalizationMap_ker` derive surjectivity and the principal kernel from the pinned localization APIs.

The generic `triple_product_not_formallySmooth` uses Mathlib's infinitesimal lifting equivalence. If that quotient were formally smooth, its actual square-zero thickening modulo the square of the presentation kernel would admit a section. Representatives of the lifted coordinates have the form `a + abc*u`, `b + abc*v`, `c + abc*w`. Multiplying them, using the lifted relation and cancelling the nonzero `abc` in the localized polynomial domain gives an identity whose evaluation at a point with all three pairwise products zero is `1=0`. This establishes the nonsmooth direction without assuming a Jacobian criterion, a regularity theorem or the desired smoothness characterization.

For the converse, evaluation-preserving substitution sets the unique zero coordinate to zero. This polynomial map kills the relation and extends through the quotient and every denominator in the actual prime complement. The other two coordinates are units in the actual local ring, so their product forces the remaining coordinate to vanish there. Consequently the chart gives an actual algebra retraction from the formally smooth localized polynomial ring. `formallySmooth_of_retraction` proves descent of formal smoothness using the genuine infinitesimal lifting property. The construction covers each of the three choices of zero coordinate.

The separately implemented `Algebra.lean` supplies the actual reduced coordinate-ring isomorphism, including its Nullstellensatz and squarefree-ideal proof. The root's sealed `SmoothTransport.lean` transports the genuine local smooth locus along an algebra equivalence. `reducedPoint_comap` identifies the transported evaluation prime in all nine original coordinates, and injectivity of the quotient-prime comap identifies any prime allowed by the original definition. Thus the final contract applies to the original matrix-space predicate, rather than only to an auxiliary quotient.
