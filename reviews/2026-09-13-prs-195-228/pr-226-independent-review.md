### Proper approximation and the upper bounds

1. **The optimization target is the actual HODLR optimum.** Ordered sibling rectangles and final leaf blocks partition the entries. Consequently squared Frobenius error separates into the actual truncated-SVD tails on those rectangles, and every proper candidate has nonnegative excess in each block. Size-2k terminal diagonal blocks are legitimately unrestricted because their internal k-by-k sibling blocks automatically have rank at most k. Exact reconstruction uses n charged basis queries and achieves the true optimum.

2. **The rank-truncation perturbation inequality needs no hidden gap.** Rechecked its variational comparison of right singular projectors, the bound `||Q-Q0||_F <= sqrt(2k)`, the identity `Z^T G = T^T G`, and the final orthogonal error decomposition. The claimed coefficients follow without assuming independence, a spectral gap, or a unique singular subspace. Its row-isotropic corollary uses only the displayed second moments; low-dimensional and zero-dimensional fitted spaces are accounted for.

3. **Noisy Gaussian block fitting uses the appropriate conditional independence.** The top-k Gaussian coordinates in the right sketch are independent of both tail coordinates and contamination. Conditional on the right range, the left design is independent of the projected residual and the independent left contamination. The inverse-Gram expectation has denominator `t-r-1`; replacing it by the weaker deterministic denominator `t-s-1` is in the correct direction. Projection to a retained space, followed by rank-k truncation, produces a proper block. Neither a free exact block product nor an exact best residual is used by the algorithm.

4. **Peeling counts and dependencies close.** The coarser pilot is frozen across both orientations at a level. After conditioning on it, each contaminating coarser entry is counted at most once in each of the two relevant sums, with collision expectation `1/B`. Fresh right and left Gaussians therefore fit the noisy block lemma. The global recurrence is summed over expected residuals, with its mixed terms controlled by Cauchy–Schwarz; the chosen parameters leave a positive denominator. The separate terminal regression is charged. These arguments give the preliminary bound and the constant-accuracy pilot needed next.

5. **Shared fitting does not assume independent column errors.** For a fixed retained block, the same embedded basis occurs in every relevant column design. Its Gaussian image is jointly independent of *all* other design components and residual-noise images used for these columns, since each is orthogonal to that basis. Rotating this common image rightward preserves the joint distribution and rotates the coefficient-error matrix leftward. Sign flips and permutations consequently establish row-isotropic cross-column second moments. Individual least-squares moments determine their diagonal trace, even though different column errors may be correlated. This supplies precisely the hypothesis of the truncation corollary.

6. **The final risk and query orders follow.** A column participates in one ordered sibling column interval at each of `h=L-1` levels, so the sum of the block covariance traces is at most `h R_Q^2/d`. The retained-space ideal proper error bounds the larger linear-model projection residual. Summation and Jensen give expected squared error at most `(1+6 eta) OPT^2`, with `eta=epsilon/500`. Properness makes excess nonnegative, giving the stated fixed-input failure bound below 0.01, including zero-optimum inputs. Pilot widths and the final sketch width are deterministic functions of the original parameters; all query vectors can be drawn in advance. The resulting budget is `2h^2(s+t)+p`, of order `kL^2/epsilon + kL/epsilon^2`, with the legitimate n-query fallback. The separate known-space theorem is explicitly conditional and is not used as a free premise for the general upper bound.

### The adaptive lower bounds

7. **Exact-recovery obstruction applies to measurable algorithms.** The stated HODLR linear subspace has dimension `nk(L+1)`. At most n scalar linear measurements arrive per query. Conditional on any seed and history, the next measurement is fixed, so a Gaussian prior retains a non-atomic posterior whenever fewer than `k(L+1)` queries have been made. Since the optimum is zero on that prior, this excludes positive average exact success and therefore excludes the pointwise 0.99 guarantee.

8. **The Gaussian transcript arguments cover both sides and adaptivity.** For iid rectangular noise, keep separate orthonormal forward and transpose query spans. The unexposed Gaussian rectangle stays conditionally centered and isotropic; newly exposed rows or columns are independent of what remains. The old-span portion of an answer is determined by earlier answers. At a common history the conditional means of the fresh portion differ by the projected fixed shift, yielding at most `delta^2/2` divergence per query. For the symmetric GOE family both query sides coincide, and the unexposed symmetric compression has its original `1/n` normalization. Its new-column covariance is `(P_perp+vv^T)/n`, giving the bound `n delta^2/2`. Seed averaging, redundant queries, and stopping do not create extra information. Known invertible diagonal scaling is simulated query for query; the proof separately analyzes the scaled Frobenius objective rather than treating scaling as loss-invariant.

9. **The depth packing has genuine simultaneous operator-norm control.** Selected first-k column sets of right children are disjoint across levels. Fine-to-coarse orthogonalization has the stated number of descendant columns, leaving enough dimension for every new frame. The nonzero global columns are orthonormal, so every shift has operator norm one. Weighted sphere-cap bounds hold uniformly after finer frames are fixed, making the coarse-to-fine integration of small-ball probabilities valid. The greedy finite packing has the claimed entropy and weighted separation. This is a packing of the full hierarchical family, not unrelated blockwise packings whose operator norm grows with depth.

10. **Anchors convert actual approximation excess to decoding accuracy.** Fixed anchors and frame shifts occupy only k selected columns per target block, so the clean weighted matrix is proper HODLR. Large deterministic anchors give the required least singular values on the stated good-noise event. The selected-column lemma holds for arbitrary rank-at-most-k output blocks: full rank of their selected columns forces all their other columns into the same space. Summed nonnegative local excess controls selected errors. The anchor size, weighting, noise expectations, decoding radius and entropy threshold give the claimed contradiction for `h=L-4>=4` and `epsilon h>=1`.

11. **The accuracy construction uses rank-constrained blocks, not free leaves.** It places projector choices in genuine size-2k sibling blocks of size-4k nodes. The local loss inequality permits nonsymmetric and rank-deficient output blocks, and its containing rank-k projector has a measurable selection rule. The sign/graph construction and product code provide the claimed finite projector packing for every k, including k=1,2,3. The GOE noise event, clean optimum, local excess sum and gap `delta=2^24 epsilon` imply successful finite decoding in the core regime. Its entropy contradicts the adaptive GOE information bound at the stated query threshold.

12. **Auxiliary regimes are removed.** The accuracy bound extends through monotonicity at `sqrt(k/n)` and the exact-recovery obstruction. In the depth core, `h>=L/2` and the uncapped depth term is below n; outside it, that term is at most four times the accuracy term. Combining the two capped lower bounds yields the displayed all-parameter lower bound. Thus the original small depths, k=1, very small epsilon, near-full-recovery sizes, and zero-optimum inputs are covered. The two-sketch nullspace example is correctly labeled as an observation against a simultaneous embedding shortcut; its witness may depend on the realized sketches and is not substituted for a pointwise randomized lower bound.
